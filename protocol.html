Proposal 1 · Update protocol templates

What changes, why it matters, and wording for template owners

Download complete PDF

Why update the templates?

The associations workflow produces a Summary of Evidence table (SET). The hypothesis-generating workflow already recommends a DAG and produces a Summary of Evidence on Mediators (SEM). The amendments connect the causal diagram, assumption register, and meeting record across both routes.

The retrospective TAAR1 example motivates the proposed changes:

Gap illustrated by the retrospective example What the amendment addresses
Association and mechanism questions can occur in the same review. Declare the review mode and the diagram’s purpose.
Pathways may be reconstructed after the findings. Record the proposed structure and challenge conditions before synthesis.
The two template routes produce different evidence summaries. Link both routes to the DAG and assumption register.
Meeting reasoning may remain implicit. Preserve the assumptions, judgements, and revisions in a reusable record.

What changes in each template?

The uploaded drafts frame all additions as recommendations. These practical prompts are a main methods contribution for review.

Template Where the additions appear Practical change proposed
Associations v3.2 draft Research questions; reporting bias; evidence summary; triangulation meeting. Declare the review mode, introduce a versioned DAG and register, record bias direction, and use the assumptions in meeting conclusions.

  • Associations v3.2 draft
  • Hypothesis-generating v2.2 draft

Research questions

Review mode and causal structure (recommended):

We recommend that you state the primary mode of the review: evaluating associations, hypothesis-generating, or a mixture of both. If any research question concerns mechanisms of action or hypothesised causal pathways, we recommend declaring this explicitly as a secondary (hypothesis-generating) mode.

We recommend that, in that case, you draw a scoping directed acyclic graph (DAG) at protocol stage: the variables of interest, the hypothesised directional links between them (including candidate mediators), and the outcome(s). The DAG is a working hypothesis, not a finding, and should be versioned (dated and archived).

We recommend that research questions about causal pathways are then answered edge by edge against the pre-specified DAG (i.e. “is the evidence consistent with this hypothesised link?”), rather than by reconstructing pathways after the findings.

Reporting bias

We recommend that, for each major bias, the likely direction of the resulting error is recorded relative to the relevant DAG edge (towards null, away from null, or unpredictable), in addition to the risk-of-bias judgement. Where quantitative bias adjustment is planned, the bias type can also be recorded: offset (additive) bias shifts every estimate by a roughly fixed amount and can create an effect where the true effect is zero, whereas scaling (proportional) bias inflates or shrinks estimates in proportion to their size and cannot.

Summary of the evidence

Making the Summary of Evidence (SET) table DAG-compatible (recommended):

We recommend adding the following fields to each row of the Summary of Evidence (SET) table: (i) which DAG edge(s) the evidence stream informs; (ii) up to three key assumptions on which the estimate depends, each typed as structural, identifying, indirectness or proxy, with status (agreed / contested / uncertain); (iii) the expected direction of each major bias relative to the relevant DAG edge (towards null / away from null / unpredictable); and (iv) a link to the versioned DAG and assumption register.

We recommend that the assumption register is seeded at protocol stage and versioned. Each entry records the assumption, its type, the evidence streams it concerns, its status, and what evidence would challenge it.

Triangulation meeting

Using the DAG and assumption register at the triangulation meeting (recommended):

We recommend that, before the meeting, each SET table row is mapped onto the DAG edge(s) it informs. Streams that inform different edges do not directly agree or disagree; any statement about agreement between them traverses an assumption (e.g. a proxy assumption) that should be named.

We recommend that conclusions are reported per DAG edge, with the traversed assumptions stated, rather than as a single global judgement.

We recommend that orthogonality is judged from the directions of the streams' major biases: streams whose biases favour findings in opposite directions (one towards null, one away from null) make agreement between them strongly informative. Representing each bias as a factor in the DAG makes this a checkable claim (the biases should share no common cause).

We recommend applying three revision triggers during the meeting: (a) evidence incompatible with a DAG edge; (b) an evidence stream that maps onto no edge; (c) an assumption challenged in discussion. Each trigger should lead to a versioned update of the DAG or assumption register.

DAG and assumption register

Versioning the DAG and attaching an assumption register (recommended):

We recommend that the DAG is treated as a versioned working model: it will be refined as evidence accumulates, and each version is dated and archived.

We recommend that, for each edge, you note which evidence streams (non-human, human observational, human interventional, mechanistic) can inform it.

We recommend attaching an assumption register to the DAG. For each assumption, record: its type (structural — an asserted causal link; identifying — what makes a stream's estimate causal; indirectness — how closely the stream's population, intervention and outcome match the target question, including animal-to-human relevance; proxy — a measured outcome standing in for the target outcome); its status (agreed / contested / uncertain); the streams it concerns; and what evidence would challenge it.

Data extraction

We recommend also extracting information relevant to the assumption register — for example the timing of mediator measurement, population characteristics, and intervention dose or exposure descriptors — so that indirectness and proxy assumptions can be re-evaluated as evidence accumulates, and not only effect estimates.

Summary of Evidence on Mediators (SEM)

Making the SEM DAG-compatible (recommended):

We recommend adding the following fields to the SEM for each candidate mediator: (i) which DAG edge(s) the evidence about it informs; (ii) up to three key assumptions (typed, and with status, as in the assumption register); and (iii) a link to the versioned DAG and assumption register.

Bridge to triangulation

Bridging to triangulation (recommended):

We recommend that, because this template produces a Summary of the Evidence on Mediators (SEM) rather than a Summary of Evidence (SET) table, teams preparing for triangulation derive a one-row-per-stream SET-style table from the DAG (using the fields described in the associations template) so that both template routes feed the triangulation meeting comparably.

Consensus meeting

Reviewing the assumption register at the consensus meeting (recommended):

We recommend that the assumption register is a standing agenda item: assumptions challenged by the accumulated evidence are re-statused (agreed / contested / uncertain), and the DAG is revised if a revision trigger fires (evidence incompatible with an edge; a stream that maps onto no edge; an assumption challenged in discussion).

We recommend that conclusions are reported per DAG edge, naming the assumptions they traverse. Note that a small mechanistic study that measures a node on a DAG edge can have probative value out of proportion to its precision, because it bears on whether an association is causal rather than confounded.

Link to the evidence summary

The six existing SET columns stay unchanged. One added Key Assumptions column records the relevant relationships, typed assumptions and status, and links to the versioned DAG and register. Bias direction remains in the existing bias columns. See the worked SET and template.

For a review already underway, record when the framework was introduced and which evidence was already known. LSR8 illustrates this timing distinction.
