Proposal 3 · Reporting and the Summary of Evidence table

A short evidence table that makes the assumptions behind the conclusion visible

Download complete PDF

The idea

The Summary of Evidence table (SET) keeps its existing columns for results, bias, indirectness, and other considerations. The proposed addition is a Key Assumptions column containing short typed statements or IDs, their status, and links to the DAG and assumption register. The register holds the detailed reasoning.

Worked example: TAAR1

The retrospective example shows why the measured animal outcome and the human symptom outcome need separate rows. Their relationship is the contested proxy assumption A1, shown as a dashed connector in the TAAR1 diagram.

The six existing SET headings are retained. Key Assumptions is the only added column, shaded to distinguish it from the original fields.

Estimates are followed by 95% confidence intervals.

SMD means standardised mean difference: an effect expressed in standard-deviation units. The outcomes differ, so these estimates do not represent the same target quantity.

Proposed meeting wording: “The streams inform different outcomes. Their combined interpretation for human symptoms depends on the relevance of the animal proxy and the bias and applicability of each contribution.”

Proposed template

Keep the six existing column headings from the uploaded associations template. Add Key Assumptions as the final column, shaded below. Use one row per evidence stream for the outcome under discussion.

Source of evidence Summary of the association Bias due to study limitations Bias due to reporting bias Indirectness: population/ species/ model Other considerations Key Assumptions
Review / evidence stream. Outcome, contrast, estimate and uncertainty, or qualitative summary. Within-study risk of bias and expected direction. Reporting-bias assessment and expected direction. Relevance to the target population, species, model, exposure, or outcome. Relevant issues not covered elsewhere. Relationship informed; up to three typed assumptions and status; links to the versioned DAG and register.

Below the table: state the meeting conclusion, the assumptions it depends on, and unresolved questions. The existing bias and indirectness columns retain their roles; the added column connects those judgements to the causal model.

Alignment is recorded below the table, not in it. The Key Assumptions column holds only the relationship informed, typed assumption IDs with status, and links. Below the table, one short narrative record per stream row asks whether the stream estimated the review’s target quantity at all — a different judgement from indirectness, which asks whether the estimate applies to the target population, exposure or outcome. A stream can be low in indirectness for its own estimand yet misaligned with the review target (the TAAR1 animal behavioural outcome), or closely aligned in quantity yet indirect for the target population.

Worked alignment narrative — TAAR1 animal stream

Illustrative narrative record — TAAR1 animal stream (retrospective reconstruction, for calibration only; not a completed assessment):

SET row and target reference: TAAR1 animal experimental evidence; target version [draft specification, Component A §A5].

Stream-level estimand: Effect of [compound] versus vehicle on reversal of challenge-induced hyperlocomotion in [strains/paradigms as included], reported as [SMD].

What is being summarised? Experimental contrasts across animal studies; the synthesis summarises a distribution of animal-specific effects, not the human clinical quantity.

Main points of alignment or difference: Different population (species, challenge state), different intervention (preclinical dosing), different outcome (locomotor behaviour versus PANSS-assessed symptoms), different effect definition (experimental contrast, not a treatment-policy effect).

Why this evidence contributes: Related-effect and mechanistic contribution; informs the proposed pathway and the behavioural-proxy assumption rather than the clinical target directly.

What must be assumed to connect it to the target? A1 (behavioural proxy validity, contested) and A2 (dose/compound applicability, contested).

Unresolved questions for triangulation: Whether proxy validity can be evaluated empirically; whether shared biases (for example, publication practices or selective outcome reporting operating across species) could produce concordant directions.

Reporting the meeting outcome

The proposed wording makes the conditional interpretation explicit. For TAAR1, the key point is that a judgement about human symptoms cannot treat the animal proxy as the same measured outcome.

Situation Suggested sentence structure
Contributions support the same interpretation “Evidence from [streams] is consistent with [interpretation], conditional on [assumptions].”
Agreement may reflect shared bias “The findings agree, but shared bias through [mechanism] remains a possible explanation.”
Related but non-identical effects (integration level B) “The [animal] and [human] streams address related but non-identical outcomes; their estimates are compared but not pooled, and their consistency is conditional on [assumption].”
Complementary contributions (integration level C) “The streams inform different links in the causal pathway and are interpreted as complementary; together they [support / leave uncertain] the proposed explanation, conditional on [assumptions].”
Contributions diverge “The findings diverge for [relationship]; differences in [estimands, applicability, or bias] remain unresolved.”

Preserving magnitude and uncertainty. Conclusions about agreement or divergence must preserve or point to effect magnitude and uncertainty (for example, the estimate with its confidence interval), not direction alone; a summary estimate should not be reduced to a qualitative claim such as “supports efficacy”.
