Proposal 2 · Triangulation meeting worksheet

Four steps and a common record of the judgement

Download complete PDF

Purpose

The worksheet gives the panel a common record of how evidence changes its interpretation of the DAG and its assumptions. The Summary of Evidence table (SET) summarises the evidence; the assumption register records the conditions attached to the interpretation.

See the TAAR1 DAG and register for the worked illustration. The worksheet below turns that reasoning into prompts a meeting can use.

Four steps

Step What the team records
1. Map The relationship or query each stream informs, the estimate or finding, and the assumptions needed to connect it to the target question.
2. Compare Whether each stream addresses the target causal question, a related question, or a different link (causal alignment assessment); which integration level that supports — A common effect, B related but non-identical, C different links; where findings agree, diverge, or complement.
3. Examine bias Shared and differing bias mechanisms, their expected directions, magnitudes, and uncertainty, and the implications for interpretation.
4. Revise and record The conclusion and its conditions; edge statuses for revised relationships (supported / unsupported / uncertain / not adequately investigated); changes to assumptions or the diagram; unresolved questions and actions.

Proposed worksheet fields

Before the discussion After the discussion
Target question and target specification version; date and participants; diagram, register, and SET versions; evidence already known when the framework was adopted. Conclusion and conditions; integration level chosen and its rationale; edge statuses for revised relationships; revised versions; actions, owners, and review dates.

Causal alignment assessment within the four steps

Companion guidance (Component B) supplies the questions and records within each step — no new procedure is needed. Each evidence stream gets a stream-level estimand (the quantity it can estimate in its own terms) prepared before the meeting, so Step 2 compares specified quantities rather than a study with an underspecified ambition.

Step Component B support Artefact produced
Preparation Retrieve the versioned target specification; draft stream-level estimands. Draft stream-level estimands in the meeting pack
1. Map Place each contribution on the edge, node, proxy connection or assumption it informs — not automatically a single arrow; classify contribution type. Contribution map with register IDs
2. Compare Alignment discussion: did streams estimate the same quantity? Resolve the integration level (A common effect / B related but non-identical / C different links). Distinct from the SET’s indirectness column (applicability). Below-SET narrative record per stream (draft)
3. Examine bias Identification, indirectness, transportability; then the orthogonality check — shared vs differing bias mechanisms relative to each stream’s own estimand. Orthogonality judgement; bias notes keyed to register IDs
4. Revise and record Conditional interpretation; route each change as alignment re-interpretation, DAG/register update, or formal target amendment. Final narrative records; versioned updates

Steps 1 and 2 iterate rather than sequence strictly: alignment assessment comes before or alongside mapping, never after cross-stream agreement is declared.

Alignment questions, descriptors, narrative record, and orthogonality check

Opening alignment questions (B1a)

  • What exactly was the review target, and which version of that specification is being used?
  • What did the included studies aim to estimate, and can that be inferred confidently from the reports?
  • What does the synthesis summarise: a common effect, a distribution of study effects, an association, a qualitative pattern, or several distinct results?
  • What quantity did this stream target in its own terms (the stream-level estimand)? Alignment is judged between two specified quantities, not between a study and an underspecified ambition.
  • Does the evidence provide an estimate of the target or a contribution that must be interpreted through another assumption or causal pathway?
  • Could an apparent agreement or discrepancy arise because different quantities were estimated rather than because studies yielded conflicting evidence?

When the review has no numerical target estimand (hypothesis-generating mode): the alignment question shifts from does this quantity match the target quantity? to does this evidence inform the proposed relationship, its measurement, or the assumptions connecting its components? The SEM’s rows are assessed against pathway components rather than a pooled effect.

Dimension-specific signalling questions (B1b)

Dimension Signalling questions (examples)
Population / setting Who is represented by the study and synthesis? Who is in the target? Are age, diagnosis, species, severity, context or effect modifiers different?
Intervention / exposure / comparator Are dose, timing, treatment versions, assignment versus receipt and comparison conditions the same? Is a hypothetical intervention well-defined?
Outcome / construct / measurement Is the outcome the target construct, a different manifestation or a proxy? Could changes in measurement alter direction or meaning?
Timing Do exposure period, outcome follow-up, mediator order and treatment duration match?
Quantity / causal effect definition Is the target descriptive, associative or causal? Total effect, assignment effect, hypothetical adherence effect, direct or mediated effect?
Synthesis target / summary measure What does the pooled estimate or SWiM summary target? Are measures transformable? Does pooling over studies yield the target-population quantity?
Intercurrent events (where relevant) How were discontinuation, switching, rescue treatment or competing events handled? Is this compatible with the target?

Descriptive labels (B1c) — provisional language only

  • Closely aligned: the stream addresses a compatible target quantity, notwithstanding methodological validity and applicability questions.
  • Partly aligned: substantial correspondence, but a consequential difference or qualification remains.
  • Different but relevant: the stream addresses a distinct quantity, yet can inform the target through a specified mechanism, proxy, context or assumption.
  • Connection unclear: reporting or conceptual uncertainty prevents a defensible judgement.
  • No established link to this target: currently no justified inferential connection; this does not mean the evidence has no other scientific value.

These are descriptors, not categories with validated thresholds, and should not be forced into a global rating.

From alignment judgements to the integration level

The alignment judgements resolve into the integration level for each pair or set of streams, which determines how they may be brought together:

  • A — Sufficiently common causal effect: streams estimate effects defensibly related to one target estimand → quantitative integration may be possible (transformations, transportability assumptions, bias adjustment).
  • B — Related but non-identical effects or constructs: streams address closely related questions but cannot defensibly be represented as observations of one target effect → retain within-stream quantitative synthesis; compare across streams without pooling.
  • C — Different links in a broader causal explanation: streams inform different edges or propositions in the causal model → complementary, pathway-/question-level triangulation; “agreement” is not the operative concept.

The level is a recorded judgement with its rationale, not a score; borderline or mixed cases (for example, closely aligned on intervention, different on outcome) should be described per dimension as above.

Blank narrative record (below the SET table, one per stream row)

SET row and target reference: [review, outcome, target version, synthesis version].

Stream-level estimand: [what this stream targeted in its own terms].

What is being summarised? [one to three sentences on study-level variation and the synthesis target].

Main points of alignment or difference: [key population, contrast, outcome, time and effect-definition considerations].

Why this evidence contributes: [direct/related/mechanistic/contextual/assumption-relevant contribution].

What must be assumed to connect it to the target? [DAG/register references].

Unresolved questions for triangulation: [reporting gaps, validity, transportability, bias and possible challenges].

Orthogonality check (Step 3 support)

  • List the key bias mechanisms for each stream (selection, measurement, reporting, confounding, analysis) relative to each stream’s own estimand.
  • Identify mechanisms that could operate in both streams — shared publication practices, shared measurement instruments, shared populations, or animal-to-human assumptions that transmit bias in the same direction.
  • Ask: if the shared mechanism operated, would it produce the observed pattern of agreement or disagreement?
  • Record whether the differing-bias premise of the triangulation is justified for this pair of streams, or merely asserted by design labels.

Agreement between streams with plausibly shared bias should be reported as conditional in the same way as agreement resting on a contested proxy assumption.
