About this draft

This v0.1 methodological discussion draft presents a causal-assumption framework linking a directed acyclic graph (DAG), an assumption register, and a Summary of Evidence table (SET). The templates, terminology, and reporting examples are proposals for review, not validated guidance or adopted standards. The Briefing sets out the four feedback questions.

Methodological context

Turner RM, Spiegelhalter DJ, Smith GCS, Thompson SG. Bias modelling in evidence synthesis. Journal of the Royal Statistical Society: Series A. 2009;172(1):21–47. Read the paper.

The proposed framework is a qualitative counterpart to quantitative bias modelling: it makes causal structure, stream-specific contributions, connecting assumptions, and possible shared biases explicit. It does not estimate bias parameters or replace quantitative adjustment.

Estimand layer

The estimand specification and alignment guidance draws on:

  • ICH E9(R1). Addendum on Estimands and Sensitivity Analysis in Clinical Trials. 2019. Guideline.
  • Kahan BC, Hindley J, Edwards M, Cro S, Morris TP. The estimands framework: a primer on the ICH E9(R1) addendum. BMJ. 2024;384:e076316. Read the paper.
  • Kahan BC, Devane D. Estimands: what they are and why we should use them. Journal of Clinical Epidemiology. 2026;189:112054. Read the paper.
  • Keene ON, Lynggaard H, Englert S, Lanius V, Wright D. Why estimands are needed to define treatment effects in clinical trials. BMC Medicine. 2023;21:117. Read the paper.
  • Kahan BC, Morris TP, White IR, Carpenter J, Cro S. Estimands in published protocols of randomised trials: urgent improvement needed. Trials. 2021;22:202. Read the paper.

The specification and alignment components are new methodological proposals developed alongside this framework; the full drafts are available as downloads and their development is recorded in the project’s background documents.

Applications and evidence

TAAR1: retrospective illustration

The TAAR1 example reconstructs how the framework could have supported a completed review. Its diagram, assumption IDs, and statuses are illustrative retrospective judgements, not records of the original panel using these tools.

The worked SET reports estimates and 95% confidence intervals from the published triangulation and the living systematic review. It is not a current evidence update or an independent audit of those publications.

LSR8: ongoing application

For Heat & Mental Health, the framework is being introduced after protocol completion, while the living systematic reviews are underway. Proposed assumptions and priority adjudications remain open for review; they are not completed meeting conclusions.

Templates and figures

The protocol proposals reproduce proposed additions to the associations v3.2 and hypothesis-generating v2.2 templates. Extracts and blank working templates are available on the relevant proposal pages; the original internal Word drafts are not distributed here.

The TAAR1 DAG is a simplified reconstruction highlighting selected assumptions; its proxy connection is not a physical causal arrow. The six LSR8 diagrams reproduce the draft v1.1 figures without redrawing. The deliverables schematic illustrates the proposed workflow, not a causal model. Diagrams express assumptions to examine, not established effects.
