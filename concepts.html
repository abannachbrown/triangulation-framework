Concepts: the terms needed for this proposal

How the diagram, register, and evidence summary connect

Download complete PDF

The argument

The proposed unit of reasoning is target relationship → stream-specific contribution → connecting assumptions. A cross-stream judgement needs to specify what each stream informs and the conditions under which its findings bear on the target question. Shared bias remains a possible explanation for apparent agreement.

The framework extends familiar within-study DAG reasoning to this between-stream setting. The DAG shows the proposed causal structure; the register records the connecting assumptions; the Summary of Evidence table (SET) links the findings to them. Its role as a qualitative counterpart to quantitative bias modelling is explained in the Briefing.

Ten terms for this discussion

Term Working definition
Causal DAG — directed acyclic graph A diagram of hypothesised direct causal influences, with no directed cycles. Both arrows and omitted arrows encode assumptions.
Assumption register A versioned record of assumptions with IDs, statements, affected relationships/streams, judgements, rationale, and what could challenge them.
Proxy A measured variable used to stand in for another construct. Its relevance needs justification; the animal behavioural outcome and human symptom outcome remain distinct.
Estimand The precisely specified quantity a question seeks: a descriptive quantity, an association parameter (marginal or adjusted), a causal contrast, or — for hypothesis-generating questions — a candidate relationship that may have no single numerical estimand. An estimand can be specified even when the available data cannot identify it.
Causal alignment assessment A structured judgement of whether the quantity a stream estimates, or the contribution it makes, addresses the review’s target causal question, a related question, or a different link in the causal pathway — and what assumptions are needed to connect it to the target. Distinct from indirectness (applicability of a quantity) and from risk of bias (validity of a quantity for its own estimand). Assessed narratively at the evidence-summary stage; not scored.
Integration level The level at which evidence streams can legitimately be integrated, determined by the causal alignment assessment: A — sufficiently common causal effect (quantitative integration may be possible); B — related but non-identical effects or constructs (retain within-stream synthesis; compare across streams without pooling); C — different links in a broader causal explanation (complementary, pathway-/question-level triangulation).
Stream-level estimand The quantity an evidence stream can estimate in its own terms: population, intervention/exposure, comparator, outcome, timing, summary quantity and effect type. Specified before alignment is judged, so that alignment relates two specified quantities — for example, an animal behavioural contrast and a human clinical contrast — rather than a study and an underspecified ambition.
Indirectness / applicability A difference between the evidence and the target population, exposure/intervention, outcome, timing, or setting that affects its relevance.
SET — Summary of Evidence table A concise account of findings, bias, applicability, and the assumptions relevant to triangulation.
Bias type — offset versus scaling Offset (additive / shift) bias adds a distortion and can move a true null. Scaling (proportional / stretch) bias multiplies an effect on the stated scale; a true zero remains zero on that scale.

Alignment is not indirectness. Indirectness asks whether a stream’s estimate applies to the review’s target population, exposure, outcome or setting — a question of applicability of a quantity. Alignment asks whether the stream estimated the review’s target quantity at all — a question of what was estimated, connected to the target only through stated assumptions. A stream can be low in indirectness for its own estimand yet misaligned with the review target (the TAAR1 animal behavioural outcome), or closely aligned in quantity yet indirect for the target population (a trial restricted to adults under 65). The Summary of Evidence table keeps the two judgements in their separate places: indirectness in its existing column, alignment in the narrative below the table.

Reference appendix

Additional methodological terms

These definitions retain the rest of the source glossary for reference.

Term Working definition
Node A variable in the diagram, such as exposure, a mediator, or an outcome.
Edge / arrow An assumed direct causal influence between two nodes. A target question can concern several paths rather than one arrow.
Direct versus total effect A direct effect excludes pathways through specified mediators; a total effect includes pathways through them. A trial’s total-effect estimate should not automatically be attached to a direct arrow.
Confounder A variable that causally influences both exposure and outcome, creating a non-causal route between them.
Effect modifier A characteristic across whose values an effect differs on a specified scale. It is not necessarily a confounder.
Mediator A variable on a causal pathway, such as exposure → mediator → outcome. Adjusting for it can change the quantity being estimated.
Collider A variable caused by two other variables. Conditioning on it can open a non-causal path.
Scoping mode versus hypothesis mode Scoping mode uses the diagram to clarify the question and evidence contributions. Hypothesis mode makes proposed pathways explicit for examination. Record the diagram’s purpose and when it was introduced.
SMD — standardised mean difference An effect expressed in standard-deviation units. Similar units do not make different outcomes or target quantities equivalent.
Bias direction — towards or away from the null How a bias is expected to move an estimate relative to the true value, for a specified contrast and scale. Direction may be uncertain or involve crossing the null.
Triangulation Drawing an interpretation across evidence contributions while examining their different and shared key sources of bias and assumptions.
Orthogonality / differing bias structures The extent to which key bias mechanisms differ across streams. Independence needs justification; different design labels alone do not establish it.
Causal-assumption framework (“scaffolding”) The proposed structure connecting the DAG, explicit assumptions, evidence summaries, meeting deliberation, and reporting.
Assumption types Four proposed categories: structural, identifying, proxy, and indirectness/applicability, defined below.
Structural assumption A claim about a causal relationship or its absence, such as the proposed dopamine pathway in TAAR1 (A3).
Identifying assumption A condition needed for a study’s estimate to identify the stated causal quantity, such as sufficient confounding control for an observational contrast.
Proxy assumption A claim that a measured proxy is informative about the target construct, such as the contested TAAR1 animal-to-human connection (A1).
Indirectness assumption A claim about the extent to which findings transfer to the target population, intervention/exposure, outcome, or setting.
Target relationship / causal query The question the review seeks to answer. It may concern an association, a total effect, a direct effect, or a mediated pathway.
Evidence stream Evidence grouped by a relevant design or inferential approach; distinct identifying assumptions can require separate streams.
Evidence contribution map A table showing what each stream can inform and which assumptions connect it to the target question.
Decision log A dated record of changes, reasons, and ownership, linking successive diagram and register versions.
Edge status The post-synthesis status of a relationship in the causal graph: supported, unsupported, uncertain, or not adequately investigated. Recorded in the decision log when the diagram is revised, paralleling the status judgements in the assumption register.
Evidential contribution type The role a stream plays relative to the target: direct target contribution, related effect, mechanistic, contextual, or assumption-testing. Classified separately from alignment and risk of bias; a stream may hold several roles, and roles are not tied to particular study designs.
Target version A dated, versioned statement of the review’s target quantity or question. Amended only when the question is intentionally redefined, with justification and a record of the evidence known at the time. An evidential challenge to a mechanism does not by itself change the target.
SEM — Summary of Evidence on Mediators The synthesis output of the hypothesis-generating route: a structured summary of evidence on candidate mediator relationships, from which a consensus meeting selects the mediators carried forward. A table, not a diagram — and not a structural equation model.
Causal graph Umbrella term for DAGs, causal DAGs, path diagrams and related visualisations of assumed relationships between variables. An arrow is a causal claim only when it is given a causal interpretation; a diagram drawn to display associations or statistical model structure functions as a causal graph only under that reading.
